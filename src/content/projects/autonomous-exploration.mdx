## Problem

Exploration means maximising the information gained about an unknown environment, usually while limiting
the energy spent doing it. Past sensor observations and future actions are tightly coupled: what the robot
has already seen dictates where it should go next. The coupling is even stronger with directional sensors
such as cameras, whose observations depend heavily on the robot's pose.

## Approach

Standard mapping algorithms provide the geometric representation of the environment. The core of the work
exploits that map to derive navigation roadmaps and exploration strategies that respect the safety
requirements imposed by the environment, while still producing high-quality observations with directional
sensors.

### Disk-Graph Probabilistic Roadmap

A sampling-based planner whose nodes are free-space bubbles. A biased sampling function expands the graph
towards known free space and frontiers, giving an RRT-like exploratory behaviour while keeping the
connectivity and flexibility of a graph.

### Skeleton Disk-Graph Roadmap

A deterministic roadmap built from the Hamilton-Jacobi skeleton of the free space. The skeleton is compressed
into a sparse graph of free-space bubbles that keeps its topology, and the bubbles enforce safety directly in
the roadmap structure. A frontier-based exploration strategy built on top of it autonomously and safely maps
complete 2D environments.

## Results

The methods were evaluated extensively in realistic simulation environments, then deployed on a ground mobile
robot with various range sensors in multiple real-world indoor experiments. The full work is described in the
PhD manuscript, defended in January 2025.

The project also covered the engineering around the algorithms:

- a custom Unity-based simulation environment;
- the design, manufacturing and integration of a two-degrees-of-freedom (pan-tilt) camera mount, used for
  targeted information acquisition during exploration;
- integration of the sensors and on-board processing on the wheeled robot.
